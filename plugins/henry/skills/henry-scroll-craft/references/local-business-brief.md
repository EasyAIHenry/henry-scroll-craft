# The local-business brief

The brief is eight short questions, asked once. It turns "make a cool site for
this shop" into decisions you can build from.

## Who this works for

A good fit has all of these:

- **A finished physical product** that is built from parts: a PC, a bike, a
  sofa, a coffee machine, a cake, a guitar.
- **Good photos already posted.** Instagram, Google Maps, a marketplace listing.
  Consistent backdrop and lighting is a strong sign.
- **No website**, or one that is a single link page.
- **Public proof**: a Google rating with a real review count, their own
  captions with specs and prices.

A poor fit: services with nothing to take apart (a lawyer, a cleaner), or a shop
whose only photos are busy, cropped or full of people.

## Pick the hero photo

This one photo becomes the last frame of the build and the image the visitor
remembers. Look through their posts and pick the one that has:

- The **whole product in frame**, with some space around it.
- A **plain backdrop**: a studio cloth, a clean wall, a counter.
- A **fixed camera**. No motion blur, no extreme wide angle.
- **The product switched on** if it has an "on" state: lights, a screen, steam.
  That gives you the peak for free.
- **Their own photo.** Never a manufacturer's render or a stock image.

Save it as `src/photo.jpg` at the largest size you can get.

## The eight questions

Ask all eight in one message. Any answer can be "you decide". When it is, write
your pick and mark it `Claude's pick`.

| # | Question | Good answer looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The business: what they sell, where, and where their photos are | "Custom gaming PCs, a shop in the north of the city. Photos on their Instagram." |
| 2 | Vibe in three to five words, with references | "Dark showroom, like their own photo backdrop." |
| 3 | The journey, in your order | "An empty case, the build under the scroll, their real builds, the facts, then booking a visit." |
| 4 | Energy: where calm, where loud | "Calm open, rising through the build, loudest at power-on, calm again, firm at the close." |
| 5 | The one moment a visitor would tell a friend about | "The PC built itself while I scrolled and lit up blue at the end." |
| 6 | What this site does that no other site does | "The parts list fills in as the parts go in." |
| 7 | The hero photo | "The white build on the wooden table, lights on." |
| 8 | The close: one action, and which public facts to show | "Book a showroom visit on WhatsApp. Google rating, same-day delivery, onsite warranty." |

## Collect the facts

Copy each fact from a public source and note where and when:

```
- Google rating <x.x> from <n> reviews. Google Maps, copied <date>.
- Same-day delivery. Their Instagram bio, copied <date>.
```

If a number is not public, it does not go on the page. A rating changes, so
check it again on the day the owner sees the site.

## A filled example (Cooked or Cracked Ep6, anonymised)

A custom PC shop in Singapore with hundreds of five-star Google reviews, an
Instagram full of studio shots, and no website.

1. **Business:** custom gaming PCs, assembled by hand. Photos and specs on their
   Instagram, rating on Google Maps.
2. **Vibe:** dark showroom. Near-black page like their photo backdrop, white
   type, one accent colour (ice blue from the build's lights).
3. **Journey:** an empty case, the build under the scroll, their real builds,
   the shop facts, then booking a showroom visit on WhatsApp.
4. **Energy:** calm open, rising through the build, loudest at power-on, calm
   through the collection, firm at the close.
5. **The one moment:** "Build, then the lights switch on."
6. **No other site:** the parts list fills in as the parts go in, and when the
   PC powers on, the whole page takes its light.
7. **Hero photo:** their white PC on a wooden table against a black cloth, lit
   blue.
8. **Close and facts:** book a visit on WhatsApp. Google rating and review
   count, same-day delivery, onsite support, hotline hours.

Feeling curve: curiosity (an empty case, the label reads 01 of 08),
anticipation (parts arrive, the label ticks up), delight (the last part goes
in, the room dims, the lights come on), a short hold, trust (seven more real
builds slide past), confidence (the facts as plain labels), resolve (one
button).

Tell-someone sentence: it's the site where you build the PC by scrolling and it
switches on at the end.
